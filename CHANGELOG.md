# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog 1.1.0](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

### Changed (ADR-0024 step 3a — working-set trim, not sufficient on its own)

- **The in-memory working set grew without limit** (measured on 2026-11-09: ~4.8 million flows and ~950,000 signals per day once indexing keeps up with Ethereum mainnet and TRON). It is trimmed every 10 minutes: flows older than 24 h and profiles/scores of wallets idle for 7 days (ages per network, in event time), signals older than 48 h once their wallet left the working set (HIGH/CRITICAL kept, so risk scores of active wallets are unchanged); watched wallets and addresses with loaded history are kept. Non-transfer event ids are capped at 500,000. New metrics `oci_working_set_size{kind}` and `oci_working_set_trim_millis`; `OCI_INGESTION_WORKING_SET_TRIM_INTERVAL=0` disables the trim. Trade-off for unwatched wallets: detectors see 24 h of flows (see the installation guide). Measured on 2026-11-10 with all retentions at 2 h: the trim slows the growth but memory is still not bounded at chain-wide volume (old generation 2.9 GB of 3 GB after 3 hours); step 3b (compact accumulators) is needed.

### Added (phase 1 — durable intelligence, ADR-0024 step 2)

- **Flows, signals and risk scores can survive a restart** (off by default, `OCI_PERSISTENCE_INTELLIGENCE_JOURNAL=true`: at chain-wide volumes — about 4.8 million flows and 950,000 signals per day measured on 2026-11-09 — the reload does not scale to the retention yet): journaled write-behind in PostgreSQL (`V9__intelligence_journal`) by decorators of the in-memory stores (the detectors keep reading memory), reloaded in the background at start-up within `OCI_PERSISTENCE_INTELLIGENCE_RETENTION` (7 days; flows rebuild wallet profiles without detection), purged daily. A full queue drops and counts records (`oci_journal_dropped_total`, warning): never silent. Metrics `oci_journal_written_total`, `oci_journal_backlog`.

### Added (phase 1 — durable indexing, ADR-0024 step 1)

- **Indexing resumes after a restart instead of skipping the downtime**: real chains persist their checkpoint (`V8__indexer_checkpoints`, last processed block + reorg-window block references). Before, a restart started again at head - 100: every block produced while the application was down was never indexed. A gap larger than `OCI_CHAINS_<NETWORK>_MAX_RESUME_GAP` (7200 blocks) restarts near the head and logs the skipped range.
- **Start-up no longer stalls live indexing**: the address-history restore steps aside twice as long as each chunk took, so it uses at most a third of the processing time (live indexing takes the lock once per event, history once per chunk, and a Java monitor is not fair). The restore takes about three times longer; both chains keep indexing meanwhile.

### Added (phase 1 — watchlist)

- **Watchlist of the organisation's own wallets** (`GET/POST/DELETE /api/v1/watchlist`, permission `watchlist:write` for ANALYST and above, `V7__watchlist`), a *Watchlist* page and a *Watch / Unwatch* button on the wallet page. Every transfer carries `wallet.watchlisted`, `counterparty.watchlisted` and `watchlist.active`; approvals carry `wallet.watchlisted` for the owner.
- **Behavioural pack rules scope to it**: `fund-splitting` 1.3.0, `suspicious-wallet` 1.1.0 and `suspicious-approval` 1.1.0 cover the whole chain while the watchlist is empty and only the watched wallets (and transfers to them for `suspicious-wallet`) once it is not. Sanctions, issuer freezes, large transfers and draining keep covering the whole chain.
- Console: the API key form offered permissions that do not exist on the server (`wallets:read`, `labels:write`, rejected at creation); it now lists the server's permissions.

### Fixed (after phase 0)

- **TRON fell behind during business hours** (TronGrid ~2.3 s and ~675 kB per block, measured 2026-11-09; indexing 6 blocks/min for 20 produced). Measured with new per-network timers (`oci_indexer_poll_seconds`, `oci_process_seconds`, `oci_process_lock_wait_seconds`): processing was not the bottleneck, the pass rhythm was. A real chain is now polled again at once while it is behind (it waited the poll interval after every short pass), indexes 10 blocks per pass (was 2, a limit from the shared-thread era), TRON fetches the next 4 confirmed blocks ahead in parallel, and the head is cached for one TRON block (3 s).
- **App killed under memory pressure**: the compose app service had no memory limit, so the JVM sized its heap from the whole Docker VM (6.2 GB of 7.7) and the kernel killed it while a test suite ran on the same host. `mem_limit` (`OCI_APP_MEMORY_LIMIT`, default 4g).
- **Stored address histories re-applied at start-up stalled both chains for minutes** (32 histories, ~26k events under the processing lock): re-applied in chunks of 200 events, releasing the lock between chunks.

### Phase 0 — consolidation (docs/strategy/FEATURE_ROADMAP.md)

- **No more silently lost notifications** (production: 5,675 dead letters "unknown connector 'default-webhook'"): the rule pack's placeholder `default-webhook` resolves to `OCI_DEFAULT_CONNECTOR` (with that connector's own type: Telegram, Slack…); without it the delivery is recorded `SKIPPED` with its reason (`V6__delivery_skipped`), never attempted and dead-lettered. The start-up log lists the enabled rules that cannot notify anyone.
- **Rules that can never fire are not enabled:** `RuleReachability` (oci-alerting) detects rules whose required fields or event types this deployment never produces. `contract-exploit` 1.0.1 ships disabled (it needs CONTRACT_CALL); `POST /rules/{id}/enable` answers 422 with the reasons; such rules are disabled at start-up with a warning; the console shows the reasons and disables the toggle; `GET /rules` exposes `unreachableReasons`. The rule tester and simulator now evaluate a disabled rule (a rule must be testable before it is enabled).
- **Wallet "Transactions" tab on real EVM networks** (was always 503: standard JSON-RPC cannot list an address's transactions): serves the transfers known to the platform (live indexing + address history), each in its own unit (`kind` TRANSFER, `id` per row), with paging.
- **`isContract` is real** on the wallet page (provider code lookup, cached), `null` when unknown; graph nodes say unknown instead of a hard-coded `false`.
- **Data-source limits are shown**: `limitations` on the wallet analysis and `GET /networks` (TRON: no historical balance, so `wallet-draining` and rapid drain do not run; EVM: internal transactions not indexed; observation warm-up).
- **Observation warm-up**: on real chains, "first seen by the platform" fields (`wallet.age_days`, `wallet.tx_count`, `counterparty.age_days`, `approval.spender_age_days`) are withheld until the chain has been observed for `OCI_INGESTION_AGE_WARMUP` (30 days): in production, 145 well-known DeFi contracts (1inch, Morpho, Across, LI.FI…) raised "young spender" alerts after 3 days, and every wallet looked new to `suspicious-wallet`.
- **`fund-splitting` 1.2.0**: more than 30 recipients in 3 minutes, minimum value per transfer, unknown tokens and mints excluded: 83% fewer alerts than 1.1.0 on the same real traffic (dry-run). Chain-wide fan-out stays common on mainnet: scoping to watched wallets is the next step.
- **`large-usdt-transfer` 1.1.0**: 10M USDT instead of 1M (measured on real TRON traffic during business hours: 1M ≈ 42 alerts/hour, 10M ≈ 2.5).
- **Noise report**: `GET /rules/noise` (alerts per rule: total, open, false positives, resolved, recent, false-positive rate) and an "Alerts 24 h" column in the console's rule list.
- **RPC failover**: `OCI_CHAINS_<NETWORK>_FALLBACK_URLS`, in the HTTP transport (per-endpoint circuit breaker and rate limiter, API key sent to the primary only); the unused `FailoverProvider` (which would have dropped balances, approvals, freezes and history) is removed.
- **Safer deployment defaults**: the application refuses to start with demo mode and a real chain together; port 8080 and the OTLP ports are published on 127.0.0.1; OpenAPI/Swagger UI off outside demo mode unless `OCI_API_DOCS_ENABLED=true`.
- **Metrics**: `/actuator/prometheus` (Micrometer) with indexing lag, reorgs, events, balance lookups, deliveries by status, rule triggers and late events; scrapable without a key from private addresses only; Grafana dashboard aligned (panels without data removed).
- **Licences**: the CI licence job is blocking (Maven via `.github/scripts/check-maven-licences.py`, npm via `license-checker`).
- **Removed**: Bitcoin and Solana from the console (no provider), six signal types that no detector emits, the PUSH connector (no transport). EMAIL stays pending an SMTP transport.
- **Docs aligned with the code**: API contract features not implemented yet are marked as such (rate limiting, Idempotency-Key, If-Match, keyset cursors, OIDC login in the console), risk example, ids, Node versions, testing strategy, data model V5/V6.

### Fixed

- **Real chains no longer stall start-up and indexing** (Ethereum mainnet + TRON, observed in production): the app stayed `OUT_OF_SERVICE` for minutes and TRON stopped indexing while Ethereum caught up. Causes and fixes:
  - real balance lookups (one synchronous RPC round trip each) are **budgeted**: none while more than `real-balance-max-lag` blocks (default 32) behind the head, at most `real-balance-max-lookups-per-block` (default 5) per block; beyond that `wallet.balance` is absent (`balanceLookupsSkipped` counted);
  - the lookups of one block are **prefetched concurrently** on virtual threads (`ChainEventSink.prepare`, called by `BlockIndexer` with all the block's events before emitting them), then processing reads them from the cache;
  - networks are **polled concurrently** (one lock per network; only event processing is serialised), on a virtual-thread scheduler; a real chain indexes `max-blocks-per-poll` blocks per pass (default 2) and yields after 2 s;
  - the first delivery flush runs off the main thread, so readiness no longer waits for the first indexing pass;
  - exposure detection is skipped when no risky label exists (`LabelRepository.hasRisky()`).
- **TRON indexing kept pace with the chain but never caught up** (each TronGrid call ~0.8 s for a full block, one block every 3 s): `TronGridProvider` reuses the block fetched by `getBlock(n)` for `getBlockActivity(n)` (memo, 2 s), fetches the block's transaction infos concurrently on a virtual thread, and caches the head for 1 s.
- **`wallet-draining` 1.2.0 — minimum balance:** only wallets holding at least 1 ETH/WETH, 3 BNB/WBNB, 10,000 TRX or 2,000 USDT/USDC/DAI before the outflow (other tokens never match: no price feed). With real balances the rule was accurate but noisy on Ethereum mainnet: exchange deposit addresses sweeping their whole small ETH balance (642 alerts in about an hour, median balance 0.026 ETH, 99% below 1.83 ETH).
- **Address history on a non-archive node** (PublicNode: "Archive requests require a personal token" beyond ~10-20k blocks): the history ended FAILED and was retried at every analysis. It now ends TRUNCATED at the node's limit, keeping and applying the recent events, with a clear message (`JsonRpcException.historyUnavailable()`: archive / pruned / missing trie node).
- **Slow start-up with stored address histories:** re-applying them (20 histories: 137 s in production) ran on the main thread before readiness; it now runs in the background.
- **Indexing from genesis after a cold start:** an unreachable provider reports a head of 0 instead of throwing, so a relative start block (`-100`) resolved to block 0 (`pruned history unavailable` errors). The chain now retries every 30 s until the head is known.
- **False "wallet drained" alerts on real chains** (Ethereum mainnet: 182 of 226 alerts within minutes): `wallet.balance` was estimated from the flows seen since the indexer start, so any wallet spending funds received before looked drained. On real networks it is now the balance reported by the provider at the end of the previous block (`BlockchainProvider.getBalanceAt`; EVM `eth_getBalance` / `balanceOf` via `eth_call`, bounded LRU cache reused within a block, lookups / cache hits / failures counted), and the field is **absent** when the provider cannot give it (error, rate limit, pruned state; TRON: TronGrid has no historical balance), so `RATIO_OF_BALANCE` and `wallet.balance` conditions do not match. `RapidDrainDetector` uses the same real balance on real networks and stays silent without one. Simulated demo chains keep their (exact) ledger balance.
- **Alerts lost their wallet and transaction after a restart** (`address` / `txHash` null, "No wallet" in the console): they were looked up in the in-memory event history. The subject of the triggering event (wallet, counterparty, transaction, block, time, field snapshot) is now stored with the alert (`V3__alert_subject`); the migration back-fills the wallet where the grouping key is the wallet, and re-indexing fills the rest.
- **Noisy demo** (hundreds of "Wallet drained" / "High USDT volume from a newly active wallet" alerts on a long-running demo): the demo ledger now gives routine simulated transfers realistic sizes (most scaled down, never more than a quarter of the sender's balance) and a realistic per-wallet rate on TRON. In-process measurement (TRON + Ethereum, 60 min seeded + 1 h live): 53 → 13 open alerts at startup, 63 → 13 after one hour, wallet-draining 8 → 2; rules unchanged; demo scenarios A-F still fully evidenced.
- EVM adapter: JSON-RPC errors returned with HTTP 400 (dRPC) are now classified like other JSON-RPC errors, and "ranges over N blocks" / "N blocks range" / "max range" answers trigger the chunk halving.

An implementation upgrade to `address(0)` is no longer treated as a renouncement.

### Security

- Risk scores can no longer be raised for free by third parties: unsolicited dust (below 10 USDT/USDC by default, `DetectionConfig.Exposure.dustThresholds`) and transfers of unverified token contracts (fake tokens whose `Transfer` events name a sanctioned sender) no longer create direct or indirect exposure. They produce a `DUSTING` anomaly signal with zero score weight instead. Previously 0.000001 USDT, or a fake-token transfer, from a sanctioned address scored the recipient 80/HIGH.
